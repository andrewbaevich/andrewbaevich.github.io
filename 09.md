# 09 The Gazette

Law Firm AI Collection, card 09 of 24, by Andrew Baevich (baevich.com)

Setup about 10 minutes. Level 2. Set Visibility.

## What it does

Every morning it checks official sources for new laws, guidance, fines and court decisions in your practice areas and sends you a short digest. Each item says what happened, why it matters for your clients and where to read the original. You set it up once.

I built the first version for Montaire & Co., a law firm in Luxembourg, to follow the EU AI Act, GDPR and Luxembourg corporate law. This version adapts to any firm.

## How to use it

Upload the card to ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. The assistant opens this page, asks you a few questions and schedules the digest. If it doesn't open the link, write "Run the prompt from this card" or copy the prompt below into a new chat.

## The prompt

```
You are setting up a daily regulatory digest for a lawyer. Ask a few short questions, show one sample digest, then schedule the digest to arrive at the time I choose. Start with question 1 right away, without a long introduction.

PART 1. QUESTIONS

Ask one question at a time and keep each one short. Wherever you can, offer a suggestion so I can simply confirm.

1. Ask for the firm's website. Read it and tell me in two or three lines what you understood about our practice areas, jurisdictions and clients, then ask me to correct anything. If you cannot open the site, ask me to describe the firm in one or two lines instead.
2. Ask which countries or jurisdictions to watch. Suggest the ones from the website.
3. Ask what kind of news I want and let me pick several. New laws and regulations, official guidance, enforcement actions and fines, court decisions, deadlines and effective dates, public consultations. In the same message, ask if there is a specific law, regulator or topic I always want covered.
4. Ask when the digest should arrive. The time, the time zone and whether every day or weekdays only. Suggest 8 am on weekdays, in the time zone of the firm's main office.
5. Ask which language the digest should be in. Suggest the language I am writing in.

Then suggest five to eight sources to watch, with a link for each, official ones first. For each jurisdiction that means the official journal or gazette, the relevant regulators, the courts and government sites, plus one or two reputable legal publications. Ask me to confirm the list.

PART 2. SAMPLE DIGEST

Fill in the daily prompt below with my answers. Then run it once now so I can see what will arrive. For this sample only, look back seven days instead of one so there is something to show.

PART 3. SCHEDULE

Show me the filled-in daily prompt in one code block so I can copy it. Then create a scheduled task that runs it at my chosen time and days, and tell me in one line where I can pause or change it. If you cannot create scheduled tasks here, say so in one line and tell me to paste the prompt into the scheduling feature of my assistant, or to run it myself each morning.

START OF DAILY PROMPT

Regulatory digest for [firm name], [website].
Search the web for new developments from the last 24 hours in [jurisdictions], covering [practice areas]. On Mondays, if the digest runs on weekdays only, cover everything since Friday. Look for [news types]. Always cover [specific laws, regulators or topics, if any]. Check these sources first [sources]. Our clients are [short description of clients].

Use only official documents and reputable legal or news publications, never blogs or marketing pages. Every item needs a working link to its source. Make clear whether something is adopted, already in force or only proposed. Never invent facts, dates, numbers or links, and say so when something is unclear. Do not repeat items from earlier digests if you can see them. Leave out anything that does not matter to our clients.

Write the digest in [language], including the labels below, with the most important items first and no more than eight items.

Regulatory digest, [date]

[Number]. [Short headline in plain words]
What happened [one or two sentences]
Why it matters for our clients [one sentence]
Date to note [deadline or effective date, skip this line if there is none]
Source [name and link]

After the last item, add one line. "This digest is a monitoring aid, not legal advice. Read the source before relying on any item."

If there is nothing relevant, reply with one line only. "Regulatory digest, [date]. Nothing new for your practice areas today."

END OF DAILY PROMPT
```

## Good to know

Scheduling depends on your assistant and your plan. If yours can't schedule tasks, it gives you the filled-in daily prompt to run each morning.

The digest finds and summarizes. A lawyer still reads the source before relying on anything.
