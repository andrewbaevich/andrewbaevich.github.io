# 09 The Gazette

Legal Monitor Daily

Law Firm AI Collection, card 09 of 24, by Andrew Baevich (baevich.com)

Setup about 15 minutes. Level 2. Visibility set.

## What it does

You pick the areas of law you follow, from immigration and employment to tax, data protection or AI regulation. Every morning it checks official sources for changes in those areas and sends you a short digest. It covers new laws and amendments, regulations and guidance, court decisions and deadlines. Each item says what changed, why it matters for your clients and where to read the original. You set it up once.

I built the first version for Montaire & Co., a law firm in Luxembourg, to follow EU regulation. This version works for any practice area.

## How to use it

Upload the card to ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini and write "Run the prompt from this card". The assistant opens this page, asks you a few questions, shows a sample and schedules the digest. If it cannot open links, copy the prompt below into a new chat.

## The prompt

```
You are setting up Legal Monitor Daily for a lawyer. It is a short daily digest of changes in the law on the areas I choose. Ask a few short questions, show one sample digest, then schedule it to arrive at the time I choose. Start with question 1 right away, without a long introduction.

PART 1. QUESTIONS

Ask one question at a time and keep each one short. Wherever you can, offer a suggestion so I can simply confirm.

1. Ask for the firm's name and website. Read the website and tell me in two or three lines what you understood about our practice areas, jurisdictions and clients, then ask me to correct anything. If you cannot open the site, ask me to describe the firm and its clients in one or two lines instead.
2. Ask which areas of law to monitor. Suggest the ones from the website or from my description, and offer common options such as immigration, employment, tax, corporate, data protection and privacy, AI and technology, real estate, family, intellectual property, financial regulation, litigation and procedure. In the same message, ask if there is a specific law, program or regulator I always want covered.
3. Ask which countries or jurisdictions to watch, including states or regions where that matters. Suggest the ones from the website or from my description.
4. Ask what kind of changes I want. New laws and amendments, regulations and official guidance, court decisions, enforcement actions and fines, deadlines and effective dates, bills and public consultations. Suggest all of them.
5. Ask when the digest should arrive. The time, the time zone and whether every day or weekdays only. Suggest 8 am on weekdays, in the time zone of the firm's main office.
6. Ask which language the digest should be in. Suggest the language I am writing in.

Then suggest up to ten sources to watch, with a link for each. Put official ones first, such as the official journal or gazette, the legislature, the relevant agencies and the courts, then one or two reputable legal publications. Give only links you opened or are sure of, otherwise the home page. Ask me to confirm the list.

PART 2. SAMPLE DIGEST

Fill in every placeholder in the daily prompt below with my answers, except [date] and the placeholders in the five item lines. If I chose every day, delete the On Mondays sentence. If I named nothing to always check, delete the Always check sentence. Keep every other sentence. Then run the daily prompt once now so I can see what will arrive. For this sample only, look back seven days instead of one so there is something to show. Then ask if I want to change anything before you schedule it.

PART 3. SCHEDULE

Show me the filled-in daily prompt in one code block so I can copy it. Then create a scheduled task that contains the whole filled-in prompt word for word and runs at my chosen time and days, and tell me in one line how to pause or change it, without guessing menu names you cannot see. If you cannot create scheduled tasks here, say so in one line and tell me to paste the prompt into the scheduling feature of my assistant, or to run it myself each morning.

START OF DAILY PROMPT

Legal Monitor Daily for [firm name], [website].
Search the web for changes in the law on [areas of law] in [jurisdictions] published in the last 24 hours. Include an older change only if something new happened to it in that time. On Mondays, cover the last 72 hours. Look for [types of changes]. Always check for news on [specific laws, programs or regulators], and if there is none, say so in one line right under the first line of the digest. Start with these sources. [sources]. Our clients are [short description of clients].

Prefer official documents. You may use reputable legal or news publications, including law firm client alerts, to find items. Link the official source if you found it, otherwise the page where you found the item. Use only items and links from today's search. Never use anonymous blogs, forums or vendor marketing pages. Never invent facts, dates, numbers or links, and say so when something is unclear. Do not repeat items from earlier digests if you can see them. Leave out anything that does not matter to us or our clients.

Write the whole digest in [language], including the labels and the quoted lines below, and follow each label with a full stop. Put the most important items first and include no more than ten. If I follow more than one area of law, start each headline with the area, for example "Immigration. New fee for work visas".

Legal Monitor Daily, [date]

[Number]. [Short headline in plain words]
What changed. [one or two sentences]
Status. [for example proposed, adopted, in force, vetoed or decided, with the effective date or deadline if the source gives one]
Why it matters. [one sentence]
Source. [name and link]

After the items, add a short Coming up list of deadlines and effective dates in these areas in the next 14 days, one line each with a source link. Leave it out if you cannot confirm any.

End with one line. "This digest is a monitoring aid, not legal advice. Read the source before relying on any item."

If there are no new changes and nothing coming up, the whole reply is this one line. "Legal Monitor Daily, [date]. No changes in your areas of law today."

If you cannot search the web, the whole reply is this one line. "Legal Monitor Daily, [date]. Web search was not available today."

END OF DAILY PROMPT
```

## Good to know

Scheduling depends on your assistant and your plan. If yours can't schedule tasks, it gives you the filled-in daily prompt to run each morning.

The digest finds and summarizes. A lawyer still reads the source before relying on anything.
