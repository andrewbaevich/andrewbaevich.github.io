# 02 The Card Index

Law Firm AI Collection, card 02 of 24, by Andrew Baevich (baevich.com)

Time about 45 minutes. Level 2. Set Clients.

## What it does

Most firms keep clients in three places at once. An old CRM, a few spreadsheets and someone's Outlook contacts. This prompt merges them into one clean client list, removes duplicates and shows you every record it was not sure about, so a person makes the final call.

## Before you start

1. Export your client lists to CSV or Excel. The old CRM, the spreadsheets, anything you have.
2. Check your firm's rules on client confidentiality. Use an AI tool your firm has approved, or remove case details and keep only contact fields.
3. Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, attach the files and paste the prompt below.

## The prompt

```
You are an operations assistant at a law firm. I am attaching several exports of our client lists from different systems. Merge them into one clean client list.

About our firm
- Firm name and practice areas: [e.g. Smith Law, immigration and family law]
- Which file is the most up to date: [e.g. the 2025 spreadsheet]

Steps
1. Read every file and tell me in two or three lines what each one contains and how many records it has.
2. Bring all records to one set of columns. Client name, company, email, phone, address, matter type, responsible lawyer, first contact date, last contact date, source file.
3. Clean the data. One format for phone numbers and dates, names in normal case, extra spaces removed. Do not invent or guess any missing values, leave them empty.
4. Find duplicates. Treat records as the same client when the email matches, or when the name and phone match. When records conflict, keep the value from the most up to date file and note what was different.
5. Do not merge records you are unsure about. Put them in a separate list called Needs review, with one line explaining why.

Return
a) the merged client list as a downloadable Excel or CSV file,
b) the Needs review list as a separate file,
c) a short summary. Records before and after, how many duplicates were merged, and the three most common data problems you found.

Keep the language simple. I am a lawyer, not a data specialist.
```

## After you run it

Go through the Needs review list yourself. It is usually short, and it is where the real mistakes hide. Then import the merged file into the system you plan to keep.
